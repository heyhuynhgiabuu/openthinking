# Wedge Positioning Examples

## Example invocation

> Run `wedge-positioning` for a product that helps solo builders make better startup decisions.
>
> Idea scorecard result: promising but too broad.
> ICP pain map result: solo builders who repeatedly start projects and abandon them after weak validation.
> Competitor context: lots of generic founder advice, productivity tools, and broad AI copilots.

## Example output

### Wedge statement
A brutal decision system for solo builders who keep starting SaaS ideas without narrowing them enough to validate properly.

### Promise statement
Help solo builders decide what to build, what to cut, how to price it, and when to kill it — before they waste months shipping the wrong product.

### Three use cases max
1. Evaluate whether a new idea is worth pursuing.
2. Narrow a vague product into a real MVP.
3. Review weekly progress and decide whether to continue or kill the project.

### Anti-positioning
- Not a founder second brain.
- Not a generic AI startup copilot.
- Not a project management tool.
- Not a marketplace of prompts.

### Why this wedge is better than broader positioning
It targets a clear repeated pain pattern, is easy to explain, and avoids competing with broad startup tooling. The narrower framing also makes the skill library feel operational instead of philosophical.

### Recommended next skill
`mvp-scope-cutter`

## Why this is a good example

It turns a broad “help founders think better” idea into a narrower promise with clear exclusions, which is exactly the kind of decision this skill should force.
