# Weekly Review and Kill Check Examples

## Example invocation

> Run `weekly-review-and-kill-check` on my current startup experiment.
>
> Weekly progress: published the repo, finished 3 skills, wrote examples, got 6 people to look at it.
> User feedback: 3 people said the idea-scorecard was useful, 2 said they wanted a hosted workflow, 1 said it still feels like content more than product.
> Usage signals: 6 installs, 3 people used more than one skill, no one returned for a second weekly review yet.
> Open questions: is the hosted layer necessary early, and is the positioning sharp enough?

## Example output

### Decision
**Continue, but revise positioning and focus on repeat usage**

### Biggest learned truth
People understand the value of the first skill, but the product is not yet proving recurring use. Right now it risks being seen as a clever skill library rather than a repeat decision system.

### Next week priority
Make one full chained flow obvious: `idea-scorecard` → `icp-pain-map` → `wedge-positioning` → `mvp-scope-cutter`.

### What to stop doing
- adding new skills
- thinking about marketplace features
- polishing broad branding language

### Drift warnings
- if users praise the idea but do not return, you are building admired content, not a product
- if you keep adding skills before proving recurrence, quality will drop and positioning will blur

## Why this is a good example

It separates activity from evidence and pushes the project toward repeatable use instead of rewarding motion for its own sake.
