# Worked Flow: Solo Builder Decision Chain

This example shows how the first OpenThinking skill chain should work end to end.

## Starting idea

A solo builder wants to create a tool that helps indie founders validate SaaS ideas before they waste months building the wrong product.

---

## Step 1 — `idea-scorecard`

### Goal
Judge whether the raw idea is worth pursuing at all.

### Result
- promising idea
- real pain exists
- monetization and repeat-usage risk are significant
- recommendation: **narrow**

### Why it matters
This prevents the builder from jumping straight into implementation with a broad, flattering story.

---

## Step 2 — `icp-pain-map`

### Goal
Pick one concrete customer and one urgent pain.

### Result
- primary ICP: solo builders who repeatedly start SaaS ideas and abandon them after weak validation
- pain: they waste weeks building weak ideas without a disciplined filter
- rejected ICPs: VC-backed startups, agencies, all founders, generic creators

### Why it matters
This forces the product away from the lazy target market of “everyone building startups.”

---

## Step 3 — `wedge-positioning`

### Goal
Choose one narrow market entry point and one clear promise.

### Result
- wedge: a brutal decision system for solo builders
- promise: decide what to build, what to cut, how to price it, and when to kill it before wasting months shipping the wrong product
- anti-positioning: not a founder second brain, not a generic copilot, not a project management tool

### Why it matters
This turns vague founder-help language into a sharper, more memorable positioning.

---

## Step 4 — `mvp-scope-cutter`

### Goal
Cut the product to the smallest real MVP.

### Result
- must-have flow: paste idea → run first skill → get structured output → follow next recommendation
- defer: community, integrations, full hosted dashboard, broad chat UI
- build cut: ship 1-2 great skills and a simple usage path first

### Why it matters
This keeps the builder from turning a sharp product into a fake platform.

---

## What this full flow teaches

- a good idea is not enough
- narrowing is the real work
- skill chaining creates more value than one-off prompt usage
- the product should win by producing clear reusable artifacts, not by sounding smart

## Core rule reminder

> Build something narrow, simple, self-evidently useful, and easy to adopt — then find a real distribution loop.
